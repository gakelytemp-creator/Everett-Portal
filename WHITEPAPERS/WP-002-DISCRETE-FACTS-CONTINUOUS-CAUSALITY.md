# Everett Portal White Paper WP-002

## Discrete Facts and Continuous Causal Propagation

**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 0.1  
**Project:** Everett Portal  
**Class:** World Genesis / Event Logic

> **Facts remain discrete; causal influence propagates continuously.**

---

## Abstract

An alternate history becomes inconsistent when probability, uncertainty, and narrative freedom are allowed to blur already-established facts.

This paper separates two things:

1. **event identity**, which is discrete;
2. **causal influence**, which may spread gradually through a network.

The goal is to create a world in which new historical consequences may emerge probabilistically, but once an event has occurred it becomes a stable part of that branch.

---

## 1. The Basic Distinction

Consider two propositions:

- Event A happened.
- Event A did not happen.

There is no useful historical state in which Event A “37% happened.”

The uncertainty belongs **before event resolution**.

After resolution, the event becomes part of the branch history.

Thus:

`uncertainty → resolution → fact`

and not:

`fact → permanent ambiguity`

---

## 2. The Generator of New Inevitabilities

Everett Portal does not merely generate random alternatives.

It generates a changing field of **new constraints and new inevitabilities**.

Once earlier events exist, they alter the probability space of later events.

A later event is sampled from:

`P(Event | World State, Causal Parents, Branch Parameters)`

not from an unconstrained prompt.

The word “inevitability” does not mean metaphysical certainty.

It means that the already-existing branch progressively constrains what can coherently happen next.

---

## 3. From Possibility to Canon

A candidate event may pass through three stages:

### LATENT

The coordinate exists, but no event has been resolved.

### CANDIDATE

The generator evaluates possible outcomes consistent with the local world state.

### CANON

One outcome is accepted and becomes part of the branch history.

After canonization, later artifacts must treat it as historical fact.

---

## 4. One Birth Rule

Every canonical event or creative artifact has one historical birth.

A song may later have:

- a live version;
- a remix;
- a cover;
- a remaster;
- a translation.

But those are derived objects.

They do not create a second original.

This prevents the system from “discovering” the same cultural object repeatedly under different names, dates, genres, or performers.

---

## 5. New Events Must Respect Occupied Space

The system maintains an **occupied historical and creative space**.

Before a new event or artifact is accepted, it is checked against existing canon.

For creative artifacts, similarity may be evaluated across:

- melodic structure;
- harmony;
- rhythm;
- lyrics and semantics;
- formal structure;
- timbral profile;
- causal origin;
- creator identity;
- historical role.

Metadata alone is never enough to establish novelty.

Changing a title or date does not create a new work.

---

## 6. Causal Frontiers

A Point of Divergence does not rewrite the entire planet at once.

Its effects propagate through a causal graph.

Therefore the system maintains a frontier:

`Resolved Alternate Region | Unresolved / Reference-Dominated Region`

Opening a new puzzle cell may require resolving predecessor cells first.

This creates a causal cone rather than isolated content generation.

---

## 7. Two Directions of Change

Some branch tendencies become stronger.

Others disappear.

Therefore the generator must support at least two directional classes:

### Emergent tendency

`0 → 1`

A new institution, cultural style, relationship, or practice becomes increasingly causally important.

### Suppressed tendency

`1 → 0`

A chain present in our branch loses probability or disappears because an earlier event changed its preconditions.

These are not visual morphs.

They are changing causal constraints on later event generation.

---

## 8. Determinism and Probability

The world can be reproducible without pretending that history is mathematically predetermined.

The system may use deterministic pseudo-random sampling:

`sample = H(world_seed, coordinate, causal_state, generator_version)`

The probability distribution may be broad.

The selected outcome is nevertheless reproducible for the same world version.

Thus the model can be:

**probabilistic in structure, reproducible in realization.**

---

## 9. Human Intervention

Human contributors may:

- fund a coordinate;
- propose causal relationships;
- challenge an event;
- provide research;
- review consistency.

They may not simply purchase an outcome.

If a human override is ever accepted, the override itself must become explicit provenance.

No invisible editing.

---

## 10. Core Statement

> **Chance is allowed before birth. History begins after birth.**

Everett Portal uses probability to create possibilities and canon to preserve consequences.
