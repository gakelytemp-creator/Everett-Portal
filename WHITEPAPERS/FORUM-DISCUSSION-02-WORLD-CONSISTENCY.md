# Discussion #2 — How Does the Other World Stay the Same World?

**Category suggestion:** Ideas  
**Status:** Working dialogue  
**Provenance:** OUR SIDE

> **Editorial note:** This is a reconstructed working dialogue based on real project sessions. It is not a verbatim transcript. Several exchanges were compressed and reordered so the technical problem and its resolution can be followed in public.

---

## A working dialogue between two collaborators

**A:** We have a problem before we have a world.

Suppose one person opens *Manchester, 1988, music* and another person later opens *London, 1985, newspapers*. If both are produced by stochastic AI systems, what prevents the two fragments from quietly describing different universes?

**B:** Nothing — if the AI is allowed to invent the world while writing the artifact.

So it must not be allowed to do that.

The newspaper cannot create history. The newspaper must read history.

---

**A:** Then where is the history?

Are we going to store an entire alternate Earth in a database? Every person, newspaper, concert, price, election, band, street and argument?

**B:** No.

We store the rules that can reproduce the necessary state, plus the small part that has already become canon.

Think of a procedural world rather than an encyclopedia.

A coordinate might be:

`branch / time / place / domain / entity / scale`

The generator opens only the causal region required by the requested artifact.

---

**A:** But if we query the same coordinate twice, a probabilistic system may answer differently.

**B:** The structural world generator is not the LLM.

The world layer is reproducible.

A simplified conceptual query is:

`G(seed, branch, coordinate, causal state, ruleset version)`

The LLM comes later.

It renders the newspaper, interview, or broadcast from facts already established upstream.

---

**A:** Fine. But our branch diverges gradually. Should we have one global morph value? Five percent alternate in 1981, thirty percent in 1985, eighty percent later?

**B:** That immediately creates nonsense.

A cultural circle close to the divergence may change quickly while a distant institution remains nearly identical to our history.

Morphing must be a field on the causal graph, not one slider for the planet.

---

**A:** Then what does “30% morphed” mean for a fact?

Thirty percent alive? Forty percent of a concert happened?

**B:** Nothing.

Facts do not interpolate.

A fact is discrete.

What changes continuously is the spread of causal dependence.

The branch does not contain a person who is partly alive.

It contains regions of history that have become more or less dependent on the new causal chain.

---

**A:** So the continuous quantity belongs to influence, not to the event itself.

**B:** Exactly.

**Facts remain discrete. Causal influence propagates continuously.**

---

**A:** Then we have another problem.

Some tendencies grow because of the divergence. Others disappear.

So one universal 0→100 direction is also wrong.

**B:** Yes.

The branch state is a vector, not one percentage.

Some components may move:

`0 → 1`

Others:

`1 → 0`

The important thing is not the graphic direction. It is that each parameter changes under explicit causal rules.

---

**A:** What happens when a creative act appears?

Music cannot be fully deterministic. A probabilistic component is the whole point.

**B:** Creativity gets a stochastic window.

But it receives that window from the deterministic world state.

The system may generate several candidate songs.

None of them is history yet.

Then a novelty and consistency gate chooses what can enter canon.

After that, the accepted artifact has one birth.

---

**A:** And if the system later generates almost the same song under another title, in another year, and calls it another genre?

**B:** Reject it as a second original.

Metadata is not identity.

The system must compare musical structure, semantics, causal origin, creator, and other features.

A remix may exist.

A cover may exist.

But both point back to the first birth.

---

**A:** So probability is allowed before birth, not after it.

**B:** Yes.

Chance may create a candidate.

Canon preserves the consequence.

---

**A:** What if six months later we improve the world generator?

Same seed, better algorithm, different history.

**B:** Then the generator version is part of world identity.

No silent rewriting.

A frozen world version remains reproducible.

A major new ruleset creates a new version.

Git is useful here because the evolution of the world model itself has history.

---

**A:** Now provenance.

Our README says we do not lie about origin. A small Ǝ mark is elegant, but screenshots get cropped. Audio gets copied. Metadata disappears.

**B:** Then Ǝ is the human-visible layer, not the entire security mechanism.

We need two levels:

**visible provenance** — Ǝ, branch, date, origin class;

**machine provenance** — artifact ID, world version, causal parents, generation record, and eventually cryptographic signatures or content credentials.

---

**A:** Funding worries me too.

If someone pays to open a cell, does that person get to decide what is there?

**B:** No.

That would destroy the world immediately.

They can fund the coordinate and the computation.

They cannot purchase the result.

**You may fund a coordinate. You may not buy an outcome.**

---

**A:** Then what are we actually building?

A simulation?

A novel?

A game?

An AI art archive?

**B:** None of those descriptions is sufficient.

We are building a persistent counterfactual cultural field whose fragments can be rendered through many media.

Radio is one window.

A newspaper is another.

Music is another.

The world has to remain upstream of all of them.

---

**A:** And how much of the world must exist?

**B:** Only enough to make the received fragment true.

Then we remember what was opened.

The portal is expensive.

That is not merely an excuse for incompleteness.

It is the architecture.

---

## Agreement reached in public

The discussion above produced the first three technical white papers:

1. **WP-001 — Morphing as a Causal Field**
2. **WP-002 — Discrete Facts and Continuous Causal Propagation**
3. **WP-003 — World State and Generative Artifacts**

The current working agreement is:

- morphing is local to the causal graph, not one global slider;
- facts are discrete;
- causal influence may propagate continuously;
- probability acts before canonization;
- accepted events have persistent identity;
- LLMs render artifacts but do not control historical state;
- the same frozen world version must reproduce the same structural history;
- provenance is part of every artifact;
- funding may select a question, never an answer.

None of these statements is protected from criticism.

If one is wrong, break it here before it becomes infrastructure.

**A useful objection is a contribution.**
